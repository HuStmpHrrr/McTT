Division

Contents

    A client of Prelude::Arith::Div and Prelude::Arith::Divides: computed quotients and remainders, and closed instances of their theorems. The theorems take d for the positive divisor succ d, so divModSpec 17 4 is the division equation for 17 by 5.

    module Division where

import Prelude::Arith::Div use (div; mod; divSucc; modSucc)
open Prelude::Arith::Div.Zero use (divZeroDivisor; modZeroDivisor; divZero; modZero)
open Prelude::Arith::Div.Bounds use (divModSpec; modLt; modSmall; divSmall)
open Prelude::Arith::Div.Periodic use (modSelf; modPlusDivisor)
open Prelude::Arith::Div.One use (modOne; divOne)
import Prelude::Arith::Divides use (Divides)
open Prelude::Arith::Divides.Basic use (zeroDivides; dividesRefl; dividesZero; oneDivides)
open Prelude::Arith::Divides.Closure use (dividesPlus; dividesMult)
open Prelude::Arith::Divides.Two use (evenDividesTwo; dividesTwoEven)

    17 = 3 * 5 + 2.

    eval div 17 5

eval mod 17 5

    By convention, dividing by zero gives quotient 0 and leaves the dividend as the remainder.

    eval div 17 0

eval mod 17 0

eval div 20 4

eval mod 20 4

    The implementation divides by the successor: 17 = 4 * 4 + 1.

    eval divSucc 17 3

eval modSucc 17 3

eval divZeroDivisor 17

eval modZeroDivisor 17

eval divZero 4

eval modZero 0

eval divModSpec 17 4

eval modLt 17 4

eval modSmall 3 4 true

eval divSmall 3 4 true

eval modSelf 6

eval modPlusDivisor 9 4

eval modOne 7

eval divOne 7
