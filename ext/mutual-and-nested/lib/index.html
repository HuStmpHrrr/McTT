Prelude

  • Prelude›Algebra›AbelianGroup

    Abelian groups: IsAbelianGroup A eq op e inv holds when the operations form a group up to eq, its predicate isGroup, and op is commutative.

  • Prelude›Algebra›AbelianGroup›Properties

    Laws of an abelian group A, up to A.

  • Prelude›Algebra›CommutativeMonoid

    Commutative monoids: IsCommutativeMonoid A eq op e holds when op and e form a monoid up to eq, its predicate isMonoid, and op is commutative.

  • Prelude›Algebra›CommutativeMonoid›Properties

    Laws of a commutative monoid M, up to M.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Group

    Groups: IsGroup A eq op e inv holds when op and e form a monoid up to eq, its predicate isMonoid, and inv respects eq and is an inverse on both sides.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Group›Hom

    Homomorphisms of groups: Hom G H is the record of a map from the carrier of G to that of H that respects the relations and takes products to products, up to H.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Group›Properties

    Laws of a group G, derived from the fields of Prelude›Algebra›Group and stated up to G.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Instances›Bool

    The booleans as instances of the structures of Prelude›Algebra, up to Leibniz equality, at the levels 0l and 1l: commutative monoids under and with tt (conjunction) and under or with ff (disjunction), the lattice of and and or (andOr), and the boolean ring of xor and and (ring), in which every element is its own negative.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Instances›Endo

    The functions from A to itself under composition, with the identity as the neutral element: the endomorphism monoid of A, endo, up to Leibniz equality, at the levels u and succl u.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Instances›Endo›Pointwise

    Functions on numbers under composition, compared pointwise with the equality of numbers Eq: Pointwise f g holds when f n and g n are equal for every n.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Instances›Integer

    The integers of Prelude›Data›Integer as instances of the structures of Prelude›Algebra, up to Leibniz equality, at the levels 0l and 1l: an abelian group under addition (additive) and a commutative monoid under multiplication (multiplicative).

  • Prelude›Algebra›Instances›List

    The lists over A under append, with nil as the neutral element, up to Leibniz equality: the free monoid on A, freeMonoid, at the levels u and succl u.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Instances›Nat

    The numbers as instances of the structures of Prelude›Algebra, up to the equality of numbers Eq of Prelude›Arith›Equality, which computes, so a closed law between numbers normalizes to ⋆: commutative monoids under addition (additive), multiplication (multiplicative) and maximum (maximum), the semiring of addition and multiplication (semiring), and the lattice of min and max (minMax).

  • Prelude›Algebra›Instances›Product

    The product of two monoids M and N of the same levels: pairs of Prelude›Data›Prod, combined componentwise, with the pair of the neutral elements as neutral element (product).

  • Prelude›Algebra›Integers

    The integers, as differences of numbers.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Lattice

    Lattices: IsLattice A eq meet join holds when meet and join are semilattices up to eq on the one carrier A, meetIsSemilattice and joinIsSemilattice, tied by the two absorption laws.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Lattice›Properties

    Laws of a lattice L, up to L.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Leibniz

    The Leibniz equality of Prelude›Logic›Leibniz as the relation of the structures of Prelude›Algebra: it is an equivalence (idIsEquivalence) and every operation respects it (idIsMagma), so a Leibniz instance fills its bottom predicate in one line.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Magma

    Magmas: IsMagma A eq op holds when eq is an equivalence on A and op respects it in both arguments; the bundle Magma is a carrier, a relation, an operation and that predicate.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Monoid

    Monoids: IsMonoid A eq op e holds when op is a semigroup up to eq, its predicate isSemigroup, and e is neutral on both sides; the bundle Monoid is a carrier, a relation, op, e and that predicate.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Monoid›Hom

    Homomorphisms of monoids: Hom M N is the record of a map from the carrier of M to that of N that respects the relations, takes products to products and the neutral element to the neutral element, up to N.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Monoid›Properties

    Laws of a monoid M, derived from the fields of Prelude›Algebra›Monoid and stated up to its relation M.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Ring

    Rings: IsRing A eq plus mul zero one neg holds when the operations form a semiring up to eq, isSemiring, and neg respects eq and is a left inverse of plus.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Ring›Properties

    Laws of a ring R, up to R.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Semigroup

    Semigroups: IsSemigroup A eq op holds when op is a magma up to eq, its predicate isMagma, and associative up to eq; the bundle Semigroup is a carrier, a relation, an operation and that predicate.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Semilattice

    Semilattices: IsSemilattice A eq op holds when op is a semigroup up to eq, its predicate isSemigroup, commutative and idempotent, as the minimum of two numbers or the conjunction of two booleans.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Semilattice›Properties

    The order of a semilattice S: Le x y when S.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Semiring

    Semirings: IsSemiring A eq plus mul zero one holds when plus and zero form a commutative monoid up to eq, plusIsCommutativeMonoid, and mul and one a monoid, mulIsMonoid, both on the one carrier A; mul distributes over plus on both sides, and zero absorbs on both sides.

  • Prelude›Algebra›Setoid

    Setoids: IsEquivalence A eq holds when eq is reflexive, symmetric and transitive, and the bundle Setoid is a carrier with such a relation.

  • Prelude›Arith›Ackermann

    The Ackermann function, by higher-order primitive recursion, with its defining equations and closed forms for ack 1 and ack 2.

  • Prelude›Arith›Binary

    Binary representation of numbers.

  • Prelude›Arith›Binomial

    Binomial coefficients choose n k, computed by Pascal's recursion.

  • Prelude›Arith›Binomial›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Arith›Binomial: the edges of Pascal's triangle, vanishing above the diagonal, Pascal's rule, symmetry, and the row sum sumTo (choose n) (succ n) = pow 2 n.

  • Prelude›Arith›Decide

    Decidability of equality and order on natural numbers, the boolean equality test eqb with its specification and case analysis, and the conditional pick on whether a number is zero.

  • Prelude›Arith›Div

    Euclidean division.

  • Prelude›Arith›Divides

    Divisibility, decided by the remainder of Prelude›Arith›Div, with its basic laws and its link to Prelude›Arith›Parity.

  • Prelude›Arith›Equality

    Equality of natural numbers, computed by recursion on both sides, with its basic laws.

  • Prelude›Arith›Factorial

    The factorial, with its defining equations, positivity, monotonicity in one step, and the bound pow 2 n <= factorial (succ n).

  • Prelude›Arith›Fibonacci

    The Fibonacci numbers, computed in linear time by iterating on a pair of consecutive values.

  • Prelude›Arith›Fibonacci›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Arith›Fibonacci: the defining recurrence, positivity, monotonicity, the sum identity and the addition formula.

  • Prelude›Arith›Gcd

    The greatest common divisor, by Euclid's algorithm.

  • Prelude›Arith›Gcd›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Arith›Gcd: the values at 0, 1 and the diagonal, independence from the fuel, divisibility of both arguments, the greatest divisor property, and commutativity.

  • Prelude›Arith›Induction

    Derived induction principles on natural numbers: case analysis, two-step induction and strong (course-of-values) induction, all from elim, for motives of any level.

  • Prelude›Arith›Lattice

    The natural numbers as a distributive lattice under Prelude›Arith›MinMax.

  • Prelude›Arith›MinMax

    Minimum and maximum, by recursion on both arguments, with their laws stated with Prelude›Arith›Equality.

  • Prelude›Arith›Monotone

    Monotonicity of addition, multiplication and subtraction with respect to Prelude›Arith›Order.

  • Prelude›Arith›Mult

    Multiplication and its laws, stated with Prelude›Arith›Equality and proved from the laws of Prelude›Arith›Plus.

  • Prelude›Arith›Order

    The order on natural numbers, computed by recursion on both sides like Prelude›Arith›Equality.

  • Prelude›Arith›Parity

    Parity of natural numbers: Even and Odd computed by recursion, with doubling and the basic laws, among them double n = n + n and the distribution of double over plus.

  • Prelude›Arith›Plus

    Addition and its laws, stated with Prelude›Arith›Equality.

  • Prelude›Arith›Pow

    Exponentiation by recursion on the exponent, with its laws stated with Prelude›Arith›Equality and proved from those of Prelude›Arith›Mult.

  • Prelude›Arith›Prime

    Prime numbers by trial division.

  • Prelude›Arith›Sub

    Predecessor and truncated subtraction, with their laws stated with Prelude›Arith›Equality.

  • Prelude›Arith›Sum

    Finite sums sumTo f n of f i for i < n, with linearity laws, sums of constants, and the closed forms of Gauss and of the odd numbers.

  • Prelude›Builtin›Nat

    The natural numbers, as an inductive type bound as the builtin natural numbers: once this unit is imported, directly or not, the numerals denote chains of its constructors, 2 is succ (succ zero), and the token ℕ denotes Nat.

  • Prelude›Data›Binary

    Binary natural numbers: Bin has the constructors zero, doubleSucc x for 2 (1 + x) and succDouble x for 1 + 2 x, so every number has exactly one form, of length its number of binary digits.

  • Prelude›Data›Binary›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›Binary: toNat takes suc, double and add to succ, doubling and plus; toNat and fromNat are inverse, so both are injective; and add is a commutative monoid with unit Bin.

  • Prelude›Data›Bool

    The booleans, an inductive type with the constructors tt and ff, and their connectives.

  • Prelude›Data›Bool›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›Bool, stated with the Leibniz equality Id of Prelude›Logic›Leibniz at Bool: negation is an injective involution, and and or are commutative, associative and idempotent monoids that distribute over each other and absorb each other, de Morgan's laws, the conditional, the laws of xor (with and, those of a ring), and the correctness of eqb.

  • Prelude›Data›Church

    Church encodings at the universe level u: numerals, booleans and lists.

  • Prelude›Data›Church›Levels

    Church numerals across two levels: those of Prelude›Data›Church u and of Prelude›Data›Church (succl u), one level up.

  • Prelude›Data›Church›Levels›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›Church›Levels u: exponentiation computes the power, moving between the levels u and succl u keeps the number, and isZero answers cfalse on successors.

  • Prelude›Data›Church›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›Church u: the arithmetic of numerals agrees with that of ℕ, and the length and sum of lists.

  • Prelude›Data›Integer

    The integers as an inductive type: pos n is the integer n and negsuc n is -(n + 1), so every integer has exactly one form.

  • Prelude›Data›Integer›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›Integer, stated with the Leibniz equality of Prelude›Logic›Leibniz at Int: neg is an injective involution; diff and neg; add is a commutative group with unit pos 0 and inverse neg; succInt and predInt are inverse; and mul is commutative and associative, with unit pos 1 and zero pos 0, and abs takes it to mult.

  • Prelude›Data›List

    Finite lists: List u A has the constructors nil and cons x xs, for x : A and xs : List u A, at every level u.

  • Prelude›Data›List›Nat

    Lists of numbers, List 0l ℕ of Prelude›Data›List: their sum.

  • Prelude›Data›List›Nat›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›List›Nat, stated with Prelude›Arith›Equality: sum takes append to plus and is invariant under reverse.

  • Prelude›Data›List›NonEmpty

    Non-empty lists: NonEmpty u A has the one constructor cons x xs, a head x : A and a tail xs : List u A, at every level u.

  • Prelude›Data›List›NonEmpty›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›List›NonEmpty, at every level u: toList and the concatenations, the lengths, and map.

  • Prelude›Data›List›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›List over a fixed element type A of Type@{u}.

  • Prelude›Data›Option

    Optional values: Option u A has the constructors none and some x for x : A, at every level u.

  • Prelude›Data›Option›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›Option over a fixed element type A of Type@{u}, stated with the Leibniz equality of Prelude›Logic›Leibniz: map is a functor, bind with some a monad, map is a bind, and the laws of getOr, isSome and orElse.

  • Prelude›Data›Ordering

    The result of a comparison: less, equal or greater.

  • Prelude›Data›Ordering›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›Ordering, stated with the Leibniz equality of Prelude›Logic›Leibniz at Ordering: flip is an involution and reverses compare; compare is correct, Spec m n (compare m n), and complete for each outcome; thenCmp is a monoid with unit equal.

  • Prelude›Data›Parity

    Parities: even and odd, the integers modulo 2, with the opposite parity inv, addition add and multiplication mul, the parity of a number, and the isomorphism toSign with the signs of Prelude›Data›Sign.

  • Prelude›Data›Parity›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›Parity, stated with the Leibniz equality of Prelude›Logic›Leibniz at Parity: inv is an injective involution without fixed point; add is a commutative group with unit even in which every parity is its own inverse; mul is commutative, associative and idempotent, with unit odd and zero even, and distributes over add; toSign is an isomorphism taking add to the product of signs; and parity takes succ, plus and mult to inv, add and mul.

  • Prelude›Data›Prod

    Pairs: Prod u A B is the record of a first component fst : A and a second snd : B, at every level u; pair u A B a b builds one.

  • Prelude›Data›Prod›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›Prod over fixed component types A and B of Type@{u}, stated with the Leibniz equality of Prelude›Logic›Leibniz: the projections and surjective pairing, swap is an involution, assoc and unassoc are inverse, map is a functor in both components, and currying is inverse to uncurrying.

  • Prelude›Data›Sigma

    Dependent pairs: Sigma is the record of a first component fst : A and a second snd : B fst, whose type depends on the first; pair a b builds one, for a : A and b : B a.

  • Prelude›Data›Sigma›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›Sigma u A B, stated with the Leibniz equality of Prelude›Logic›Leibniz: the projections of a pair, surjective pairing, the equality of pairs whose second component is a function of the first, and the injectivity of pair in its first component.

  • Prelude›Data›Sign

    Signs: negative and positive, with the opposite sign and the multiplication of signs, under which they form a group with unit positive.

  • Prelude›Data›Sign›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›Sign, stated with the Leibniz equality of Prelude›Logic›Leibniz at Sign: opposite is an injective involution without fixed point, and mul is a commutative group with unit positive in which every sign is its own inverse.

  • Prelude›Data›Stream

    Infinite streams over A, encoded as functions from indices: Stream is ∀ (n : ℕ) → A.

  • Prelude›Data›Stream›Nat

    Streams of numbers, Prelude›Data›Stream 0l ℕ: the stream nats of all numbers.

  • Prelude›Data›Stream›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›Stream on streams of numbers, stated pointwise with Prelude›Arith›Equality, and of the stream nats of Prelude›Data›Stream›Nat: elements of the operations, the scan by addition as prefix sums of Prelude›Arith›Sum, interleaving at even and odd indices of Prelude›Arith›Parity, and sums of taken prefixes.

  • Prelude›Data›Sum

    Disjoint unions: Sum u A B has the constructors inl a for a : A and inr b for b : B, at every level u.

  • Prelude›Data›Sum›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›Sum over fixed summands A and B of Type@{u}, stated with the Leibniz equality of Prelude›Logic›Leibniz: case with the constructors is the identity, swap is an involution, assoc and unassoc are inverse, map is a functor and case absorbs it, and swap negates isLeft.

  • Prelude›Data›These

    Inclusive unions: These u A B has the constructors this a, that b and these a b, at every level u: a value of A, of B, or of both.

  • Prelude›Data›These›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›These over fixed component types A and B of Type@{u}, stated with the Leibniz equality of Prelude›Logic›Leibniz: the constructors are injective, these in each component, and pairwise distinct, swap is an involution, map is a functor, and fold after swap or fromSum.

  • Prelude›Data›Tree›Binary

    Binary trees: Tree u N L has leaves leaf x labelled by x : L and nodes node l m r labelled by m : N, with two subtrees, at every level u.

  • Prelude›Data›Tree›Binary›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›Tree›Binary over node labels N and leaf labels L: map keeps the counts and is a functor, respecting pointwise equality of functions; a tree has one more leaf than nodes, the lists of labels have the counts as lengths, and mirror is an involution that keeps the counts and reverses toListInfix.

  • Prelude›Data›Tree›Forest

    Trees and forests, a mutual group: a tree node x ts has a label x : A and a forest ts of children, and a forest is nil or cons t ts, a tree followed by a forest.

  • Prelude›Data›Tree›Forest›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›Tree›Forest over labels A of Type@{u}: map keeps the size and is a functor; the preorder list of labels has the size as its length and commutes with map; the size and the preorder list are folds, by conversion; and toList and fromList are inverse.

  • Prelude›Data›Tree›Rose

    Rose trees, a nested inductive type: a tree node x ts has a label x : A and a list ts : List u (Rose u A) of children, the lists of Prelude›Data›List.

  • Prelude›Data›Tree›Rose›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›Tree›Rose over labels A of Type@{u}: map keeps the size, is a functor, and is List's map on forests; the preorder list of labels has the size as its length and commutes with map.

  • Prelude›Data›Vec

    Vectors of length n over A, encoded as functions from bounded indices: Vec n is ∀ (i : ℕ) (h : Lt i n) → A.

  • Prelude›Data›Vec›Nat

    Vectors of numbers, Prelude›Data›Vec 0l ℕ: their sum sumVec.

  • Prelude›Data›Vec›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›Vec, stated with Prelude›Arith›Equality on number-valued observations: elements read with nth, sums of vectors of numbers (sumVec of Prelude›Data›Vec›Nat), and with IsTrue on the answers of the boolean tests allb and anyb.

  • Prelude›Data›W

    Well-founded trees: W u A B has the one constructor sup x f, a node of shape x : A with a subtree f p at each position p : B x, at every level u.

  • Prelude›Data›W›Properties

    Laws of Prelude›Data›W, at every level u, stated with the Leibniz equality of Prelude›Logic›Leibniz: the projections of sup and surjective pairing, the injectivity of sup in its shape, map on the head, the computation of foldr, and emptiness: if every shape has a position, there is no tree.

  • Prelude›Function›Combinators

    Generic combinators over a type parameter at any universe level, with their laws at ℕ.

  • Prelude›Function›Iter

    Iteration of an arbitrary function: a unit parameterized by the level u of the type, the type A and the function, so each client instantiates it as it needs: Iter.

  • Prelude›Logic

    Propositions as types, at every universe level: ⊤ holds, ⊥ has no proof; negation, Church-encoded conjunction, disjunction, equivalence and existence over numbers, and decidability.

  • Prelude›Logic›Leibniz

    Leibniz equality of the elements of a type A of Type@{u}: Id A x y holds when every predicate of x holds of y.

  • Prelude›Universe›Formers

    Type formers at universe levels.

  • Prelude›Universe›Levels

    Universe levels as data.

Programs

  • Algebra

    A client of Prelude›Algebra›Monoid›Properties and the instances of monoids on numbers.

  • Arithmetic

    A client of the arithmetic in Prelude: closed instances of the laws of addition and multiplication, and a few computations.

  • Binary

    A client of Prelude›Arith›Binary: halves, low bits and binary digits of numbers, the number they write, the count of ones, and exponentiation by squaring.

  • ChurchPair

    Church-encoded pairs, passed as arguments to the program that uses them.

  • ChurchVector

    Church-encoded vectors, passed as arguments to the program that uses them, with a head function that is total on non-empty vectors.

  • Combinatorics

    A client of Prelude›Arith›Binomial and Prelude›Arith›Fibonacci: the first rows of Pascal's triangle and of the Fibonacci numbers, and closed instances of their laws, which normalize to ⋆.

  • DataTypes

    A client of the inductive types of Prelude›Data: comparisons, inclusive unions, non-empty lists, binary trees, signs and parities, integers, binary numbers and well-founded trees, computed on closed values, and closed instances of their laws.

  • Division

    A client of Prelude›Arith›Div and Prelude›Arith›Divides: computed quotients and remainders, and closed instances of their theorems.

  • Forests

    A client of the trees and forests of Prelude›Data›Tree›Forest: the operations on a closed tree of numbers, a function written with the group's eliminator and one with its recursor module, and closed instances of the laws.

  • GroupRecursors

    The recursor module of a group: mutual T where … end files, after the members, the module T.

  • Groups

    A client of Prelude›Algebra›Group›Properties, read in the group of integers of Prelude›Algebra›Integers, up to Same.

  • Hierarchy

    A client of the algebraic hierarchy of Prelude›Algebra: one function written once for every monoid, concat, run in the monoids of numbers, booleans, lists, endomorphisms and pairs; the derived laws of monoids, groups, lattices and rings, read at instances; and homomorphisms.

  • Induction

    A client of Prelude›Arith›Induction and Prelude›Arith›Decide: halving by strong and by two-step induction, decisions eliminated into Bool to show which branch they compute, and the equality test eqb.

  • Lattice

    A client of Prelude›Arith›Monotone and Prelude›Arith›Lattice: closed instances of the order and lattice laws normalize to ⋆, and a few computations with min, max and sub.

  • Lists

    A client of the inductive data types of Prelude›Data: lists, booleans, options, pairs, sums and dependent pairs, computed on closed values, and closed instances of their laws.

  • MutualGroups

    Groups of inductive types: mutual declarations, nested occurrences through views, eliminators with one block per member, and mutual definitions.

  • NatTheory

    A client of the Prelude library: Prelude›Arith›Equality, Prelude›Arith›Plus and Prelude›Function›Iter are separate units, loaded once each.

  • NumberTheory

    A client of Prelude›Arith›Gcd, its laws, and Prelude›Arith›Prime: greatest common divisors, the primes below 30, and closed instances of the theorems, which normalize to ⋆.

  • OrderParity

    A client of Prelude›Arith›Order and Prelude›Arith›Parity: closed instances of their theorems normalize to ⋆, and leb decides the order.

  • Polynomials

    Polynomials with natural coefficients, evaluated at a point.

  • Powers

    A client of Prelude›Arith›Pow, Prelude›Arith›Sum and Prelude›Arith›Factorial: a few computations, and closed instances of their theorems, which normalize to ⋆.

  • Programs

    A client of the programs in the Prelude library: subtraction, minimum and maximum, generic combinators, and the Ackermann function, run on closed instances.

  • Roses

    A client of the rose trees of Prelude›Data›Tree›Rose: the operations on a closed tree of numbers, whose children are a List of trees, a function written with the eliminator's two blocks, and closed instances of the laws.

  • Streams

    A client of Prelude›Data›Stream and its laws: the natural numbers, the even and odd numbers, the squares and two Fibonacci-like streams are read with nth and summed through take, and closed instances of the laws normalize to ⋆.

  • Tutorial

    A tour of the McTT surface language, one feature at a time, from the simplest to the most advanced; every example is checked and evaluated when the file is run.

  • Universes

    A client of the level-polymorphic Prelude: Prelude›Universe›Levels, Prelude›Universe›Formers, Prelude›Data›Church and its laws, and the level parameters of Prelude›Logic, Prelude›Data›Vec, Prelude›Function›Iter and Prelude›Arith›Induction.

  • Vectors

    A client of Prelude›Data›Vec and Prelude›Data›Vec›Properties: small vectors of numbers are built, mapped, zipped, appended, split, reversed, folded, read and summed, and closed instances of the laws normalize to ⋆.
