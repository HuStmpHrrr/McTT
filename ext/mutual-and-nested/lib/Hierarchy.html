Hierarchy

A client of the algebraic hierarchy of Prelude›Algebra: one function written once for every monoid, concat, run in the monoids of numbers, booleans, lists, endomorphisms and pairs; the derived laws of monoids, groups, lattices and rings, read at instances; and homomorphisms.

A structure is a record value, so it is passed to a function like any other argument, and its members are selected through it: M.op, M.e, R.additiveGroup. A monoid has one level, of its carrier and of its relation: the numbers, compared by Eq, are at 0l; the booleans, lists and functions, compared by Leibniz equality, which lives one level up, are at 1l.

Every element of a list, combined in a monoid M of any level, from the right: concat u M (cons a (cons b nil)) is M.op a (M.op b M.e). The list is at the level u of the monoid.

def concat (u : Level) (M : Prelude›Algebra›Monoid.Monoid u)
    (l : List u M.carrier) : M.carrier ≔
  elim l at z → M.carrier
  | nil ⇒ M.e
  | cons x xs, r ⇒ M.op x r
  end
end

Lists of numbers, booleans and functions; those of booleans and of functions are at 1l, the level of their monoids.

inductive NatList ≔ List 0l ℕ

def oneTwoThree : NatList ≔
  NatList.cons 1 (NatList.cons 2 (NatList.cons 3 NatList.nil))
end

def bools : List 1l Bool ≔
  List.cons 1l Bool tt (List.cons 1l Bool ff (List.nil 1l Bool))
end

2. Derived laws, at instances. The powers of the multiplicative monoid are the powers of numbers.

2^(2+3) is 2^2 * 2^3; both sides compute to 32.

theorem twoPowFive : Eq (Times.pow 2 (plus 2 3))
    (multiplicative.op (Times.pow 2 2) (Times.pow 2 3)) ≔
  Times.Power.powPlus 2 2 3
end

The integers form a group; invOp computes the negative of a sum.

import Prelude›Algebra›Group›Properties 1l Integer.additive.group as IntGroup

eval Integer.additive.inv (add (pos 2) (negsuc 4))
output
Evaluate Integer.additive.inv (add (pos 2) (negsuc 4))
  --> Prelude›Data›Integer.Int.pos 3 : Prelude›Data›Integer.Int
theorem negSum : Id Int (Integer.additive.inv (add (pos 2) (pos 3)))
    (add (Integer.additive.inv (pos 3)) (Integer.additive.inv (pos 2))) ≔
  IntGroup.Laws.invOp (pos 2) (pos 3)
end

The order of the min-semilattice of numbers: Le 2 5 is Eq (min 2 5) 2, which computes to ⊤, and in the lattice it gives max 2 5 = 5.

import Prelude›Algebra›Semilattice›Properties 0l minMax.meetSemilattice
  as MinOrder
import Prelude›Algebra›Lattice›Properties 0l minMax as MinMax

def twoLeFive : MinOrder.Le 2 5 ≔ ⋆ end

theorem maxTwoFive : Eq (minMax.join 2 5) 5 ≔
  MinMax.meetToJoin 2 5 twoLeFive
end

eval minMax.join 2 5
output
Evaluate minMax.join 2 5 --> 5 : ℕ

3. Homomorphisms. The first projection of pairs is a homomorphism of monoids, so it takes powers to powers: the first component of the cube of (2, 3) is the triple sum of 2.

import Prelude›Algebra›Monoid›Properties 0l Both.product as Pairs
import Prelude›Algebra›Monoid›Properties 0l additive.monoid as Plus

eval Pairs.pow (pair 0l ℕ ℕ 2 3) 3
output
Evaluate Pairs.pow (pair 0l ℕ ℕ 2 3) 3
  --> record Prelude›Data›Prod.Prod 0l ℕ ℕ where fst ≔ 6; snd ≔ 27 end
  : Prelude›Data›Prod.Prod 0l ℕ ℕ
theorem firstCube :
    Eq (Both.first.map (Pairs.pow (pair 0l ℕ ℕ 2 3) 3)) (Plus.pow 2 3) ≔
  (MH.Laws Both.product additive.monoid Both.first).mapPow (pair 0l ℕ ℕ 2 3) 3
end

eval (MH.compose Both.product additive.monoid additive.monoid
        (MH.identity additive.monoid) Both.first).map (pair 0l ℕ ℕ 7 8)
output
Evaluate (MH.compose Both.product additive.monoid additive.monoid
            (MH.identity additive.monoid)
           Both.first).map
           (pair 0l ℕ ℕ 7 8) --> 7 : ℕ
end
