Roses

A client of the rose trees of Prelude›Data›Tree›Rose: the operations on a closed tree of numbers, whose children are a List of trees, a function written with the eliminator's two blocks, and closed instances of the laws.

The tree 1 / | \ 2 3 5 | 4

def example : T ≔
  T.node 1 (F.cons (R.leaf 2)
             (F.cons (T.node 3 (F.cons (R.leaf 4) F.nil))
               (F.cons (R.leaf 5) F.nil)))
end

The children of a node, a list of trees: List's operations apply to them, and so do the forest functions. The eliminator of Rose has a block for the view too, unused here.

def children (t : T) : F ≔
  elim t at (z : T) -> F
  | node x ts, h ⇒ ts
  with (z : F) -> F
  | nil ⇒ F.nil
  | cons t ts, ht hts ⇒ F.nil
  end
end

The number of leaves, by the eliminator at the tree's block: a node is a leaf when its children are nil. The motive at forests says how many leaves a forest has. The inner eliminator, whose only block is at F, is List's own: the group is read off the first block.

def leaves (t : T) : ℕ ≔
  elim t at (z : T) -> ℕ
  | node x ts, h ⇒
      elim ts at (z : F) -> ℕ
      | nil ⇒ 1
      | cons s us, r ⇒ h
      end
  with (z : F) -> ℕ
  | nil ⇒ 0
  | cons s us, hs hus ⇒ plus hs hus
  end
end
