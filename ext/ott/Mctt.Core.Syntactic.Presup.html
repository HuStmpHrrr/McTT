Presupposition

Presupposition for term equality, refinement and substitution equivalence: the context is well formed, both sides are well typed, and the type is a type. Presupposition for typing is presup_exp in Core.Syntactic.System.Lemmas, derived from the mutual theorem presup_global.
These statements come after sub_eq_preserves_exp, because the congruences whose type depends on a left premise, such as application's B[Id ,, N], type their right-hand side at the type built from the right premises, and sub_eq_preserves_exp bridges the two. That lemma is about typing derivations only, so it is proved by induction on wf_exp without presupposition.
The type of a term is a type of some sort, a universe or Prop (⊢ᵗ), and so is each side of a refinement, each at its own sort.
None of the three statements needs a mutual induction:
  • the wf_exp_eq_subtyp case of presup_exp_eq gets Θ ⍮ Ξ ⍮ Γ ⊢ A' : Typeω@i from a premise rather than from presup_subtyp;
  • the wf_subtyp_refl case of presup_subtyp uses the finished presup_exp_eq.
Both rely on extra premises that Definitions carries on those two rules for this purpose.
A few of the term-equality rules need an argument of their own, all for the same reason: a congruence rule states its equation at the type built from the left premises, so the right-hand side is typed by its own rule, at its own type, and then moved to the type of the equation by wf_conv. The congruence rule for let module premises its right-hand side instead, and only its left-hand side needs the body's type to be a type. The remaining rules, including η (whose right-hand side is wf_fn_eta_expand), are hints.

Theorem presup_exp_eq : forall {Θ Ξ Γ M M' A},

The Two Sides of a Refinement

Each side of a refinement is a type, each of its own sort: that the two sorts agree is the same-sort theorem, which needs completeness. (wf_subtyp_refl's case is where presup_exp_eq is used, and the only place it is needed.)

Theorem presup_sub_eq : forall {Θ Ξ Γ Δ σ σ'},
    Θ ⍮ Ξ ⍮ Γ ⊢ˢ σ ≈ σ' : Δ ->
    ⊢ Θ ⍮ Ξ ⍮ Γ /\ Θ ⍮ Ξ ⍮ Γ ⊢ˢ σ : Δ /\ Θ ⍮ Ξ ⍮ Γ ⊢ˢ σ' : Δ /\ ⊢ Θ ⍮ Ξ ⍮ Δ.
